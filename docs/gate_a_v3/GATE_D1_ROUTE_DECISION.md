# Gate D1 route decision: no N=6 \(\nu_\pi\)-weight confirmation

## Frozen decision

Gate D1 does **not** authorize an N=6 confirmation campaign. The prospective N=4 protocol required two conditions before N=6 could be considered: both fixed-\(\nu_0\) median raw-weight ratios must exceed 10, and the smallest of the four \(\nu_\pi=1\) weights must exceed the largest of the four \(\nu_\pi=0\) weights by a factor greater than 10. The first condition passes, but the second returns only 7.004. The screen therefore fails by its declared rule.

This decision prevents a common statistical mistake. A large class-median effect, by itself, is not enough to claim that the effect is robust across preselected deep points. The response-blind N=4 screen reveals substantial heterogeneity inside the sampled BDI classes, and that heterogeneity must constrain the interpretation rather than being eliminated through a new response-informed search.

## What remains supported

Gate A v3 still supplies the principal exact N=6 result: common-preparation TLS response is strongly boundary-selective at specified BDI-labelled drives, including the held-out same-drive OBC/PBC contrast and the six-site edge-to-interior spatial profile. Gate D1 also shows that, at N=4, raw spectral weights have large fixed-\(\nu_0\) median differences when \(\nu_\pi\) changes.

Neither result establishes a universal \(\nu_\pi\)-weight law. The sample-wide all-point condition fails, and the N=4 stratified design does not put every class on a common-period line. The correct paper language remains **finite-system, drive-class-dependent, and boundary-selective**.

## Authorized next steps

No additional N=6 point, N=8/N=10 size scan, \(K\)-manifold extension, Sambe-harmonic extension, or response-informed parameter sweep is authorized by Gate D1. A future attempt to elevate \(\nu_\pi\) to a central claim would need a new physical design—preferably a drive family with genuinely matched multi-class controls or a theory that predicts an observable robust to within-class microscopic variation—and a separately committed protocol.

For the present manuscript, the productive next action is editorial: make Gate A v3's exact finite-system boundary selectivity the primary result; use Gate B/C/D negative or heterogeneous tests as transparent boundaries of the claim; and do not describe the project as general \(\pi\)-edge spectroscopy.
