\begin{abstract}
A microscopic two-level system (TLS) can act simultaneously as a coherent local probe and as a dissipative load. We study this dual role in a finite two-step Floquet Ising chain using exact sparse Floquet--Lindblad propagation. Every principal control begins from the same product state. A held-out same-drive open-versus-periodic-boundary comparison and a six-contact scan show that the frequency-resolved transverse TLS response is concentrated at the open-chain contacts. Two strictly period-matched BDI-labelled control lines, however, rule out a universal $\nu_\pi$-specified normalized line shape for the sampled drives. Independently, a response-blind eight-drive raw-weight hierarchy passes its frozen $N=4$-to-$N=6$ transfer test. Secondary mechanism scans show approximately coupling-linear response-doublet splitting over the resolved interval $g/J=0.06$--$0.12$ and nonmonotonic dissipative backaction, with strongest loading at intermediate loss and recovery under rapid TLS relaxation. The evidence therefore supports a boundary-selective finite-system readout and a tunable local load, not a thermodynamic time-crystalline phase, universal invariant spectroscopy, or common-period causal law.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Floquet systems; two-level systems; open quantum dynamics; Lindblad dynamics; finite-size response; superconducting circuits

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Local TLS defects are a ubiquitous microscopic source of coherent structure and loss in superconducting quantum devices \citep{martinis2005decoherence,muller2019tls,lisenfeld2019electric,abdurakhimov2022identification}. In a driven device, the same locality that makes a defect a source of dissipation also makes it a possible spectroscopic probe. Here ``spectroscopic'' refers operationally to the frequency-resolved response of the scanned TLS; it does not mean that one dissipative response curve directly measures a bulk invariant. This raises a finite open-system question: which TLS response features are genuinely boundary selective, and which are artifacts of initial-state choice, geometry, finite-time analysis, or drive selection?

The two-step Ising drive provides a controlled setting within standard Floquet theory \citep{shirley1965solution,sambe1973steady,bukov2015universal}. Its closed free-fermion representation can carry candidate boundary zero and $\pi$ structures \citep{kitagawa2010topological,jiang2011majorana,thakurathi2013floquet}. A local observable, however, requires a physical preparation and a nonzero local matrix element; a closed-chain label does not by itself generate a dissipative response. Moreover, in a finite passive Lindblad system, a near-$-1$ channel mode is metastable rather than the persistent spatiotemporal order required of a time-crystalline phase \citep{else2016floquet,keyserlingk2016absolute,macieszczak2016metastability,chen2024spectral}. The appropriate task is therefore to test a full-model, finite-time response under a protocol whose competing explanations are explicit.

Here we use exact sparse Floquet--Lindblad propagation of an $N=6$ Ising chain coupled to one amplitude-damped TLS. The argument has five deliberately separated layers. First, a movable-contact scan and a held-out OBC/PBC comparison establish the observable-level boundary selectivity under one physical preparation. Second, same-period BDI-labelled controls delimit what that selectivity does \emph{not} imply: the sampled normalized line shapes are not fixed by $\nu_\pi$ alone. Third, a response-blind eight-drive test asks only whether a frozen raw-weight hierarchy transfers from $N=4$ to $N=6$. Fourth, coupling- and damping-dependent scans provide local hybridization and backaction mechanisms without being used to manufacture the boundary claim. Finally, channel and multichannel calculations delimit where a prepared reduced description is informative and where it fails. This hierarchy keeps the finite response result distinct from any universal topological law.

\section{Floquet Ising chain and dissipative TLS}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Two-step chain and local probe}

The driven chain contains $N$ spin-$1/2$ sites. During one period $T=T_1+T_2$, the two unitary chain steps are
\begin{equation}
H_{ZZ}=-J\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}Z_jZ_{j+1},\qquad
H_X=-h\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}X_j,
\end{equation}
with $T_1=\beta/(2J)$, $T_2=\alpha/(2h)$, and $J=h=1$ in the reported protocol. For OBC, $\mathcal B=\{0,\ldots,N-2\}$; for PBC, the wrap-around bond is included. For PBC the bulk dispersion is
\begin{equation}
\cos(\epsilon_kT)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\cos k\sin\alpha\sin\beta.
\label{eq:bulk-dispersion}
\end{equation}
Thus $k=0$ gives $\cos(\alpha-\beta)$ and $k=\pi$ gives $\cos(\alpha+\beta)$. A zero gap closes on the even branches $\alpha-\beta=2n\pi$ at $k=0$ or $\alpha+\beta=2n\pi$ at $k=\pi$; a $\pi/T$ gap closes on the corresponding odd branches $\alpha-\beta=(2n+1)\pi$ or $\alpha+\beta=(2n+1)\pi$. A TLS of splitting $\omega_d$ is attached at contact site $x$ through transverse exchange. The full step Hamiltonians are
\begin{align}
H_1^{(x)}&=H_{ZZ}-\frac{\omega_d}{2}Z_d+g(\sigma_x^+\sigma_d^-+\sigma_x^-\sigma_d^+),\\
H_2^{(x)}&=H_X-\frac{\omega_d}{2}Z_d+g(\sigma_x^+\sigma_d^-+\sigma_x^-\sigma_d^+).
\label{eq:step-hamiltonians}
\end{align}
The closed-chain BDI pair $(\nu_0,\nu_\pi)$ labels candidate drive controls selected away from the gap-closing lines. It is used to organize the matched controls below, never to construct a dissipative initial state.

\subsection{Amplitude damping and exact one-period channel}

The TLS is coupled to a zero-temperature amplitude-damping bath during both drive steps,
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho=\cL_k^{(x)}\rho=-\ii[H_k^{(x)},\rho]+\gamma_1\cD[\sigma_d^-]\rho,
\qquad
\cD[c]\rho=c\rho c^\dagger-\tfrac12\{c^\dagger c,\rho\}.
\label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
The stroboscopic map is therefore
\begin{equation}
\cE_F^{(x)}=e^{\cL_2^{(x)}T_2}e^{\cL_1^{(x)}T_1}.
\label{eq:floquet-channel}
\end{equation}
All response data in the main text are generated from this complete Floquet--Lindblad model in sparse Liouville space. No reduced edge--TLS Hamiltonian, bare edge wavefunction, or projected coupling is used to create the principal response curves.

For the reduced-model discussion in the Supplementary Material, $x$ always denotes the physical contact site and $\ell\in\mathbb Z$ a Floquet sideband. With one fixed coupling convention,
\begin{equation}
B_{0\pi}(x,\ell)=\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T\!\dd t\,e^{\ii\ell\Omega t}\bra{u_0(t)}\sigma_x^-\ket{u_\pi(t)},\quad g_{x,\ell}=gB_{0\pi}(x,\ell),\quad \Delta_\ell=\omega_d-[(\epsilon_\pi-\epsilon_0)+\ell\Omega].
\label{eq:sideband-convention}
\end{equation}
The reduced equations use $\dot c_e=-(\kappa_e/2)c_e-\ii g_{x,\ell}c_d+f_e$ and $\dot c_d=-(\gamma_2+\ii\Delta_\ell)c_d-\ii g_{x,\ell}^*c_e$, with $\gamma_2=\gamma_1/2$ for pure TLS relaxation. Here $\kappa_e$ is a phenomenological linewidth of the retained edge-sector amplitude and $f_e$ is the effective source generated by the physical preparation and degrees of freedom omitted by the projection. Neither is fitted to generate the full-model data. Their local susceptibility gives
\begin{align}
\frac{|c_d|}{|c_e|}&=\frac{|g_{x,\ell}|}{\sqrt{\Delta_\ell^2+\gamma_2^2}},\\
\arg\frac{c_d}{c_e}&=-\frac{\pi}{2}-\arg g_{x,\ell}-\arg(\gamma_2+\ii\Delta_\ell).
\label{eq:reduced-susceptibility}
\end{align}
Adiabatic elimination of a rapidly following TLS further gives the interpretive edge-decay scale
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm TLS}(\Delta_\ell)=\frac{4|g_{x,\ell}|^2\gamma_1}{\gamma_1^2+4\Delta_\ell^2},
\label{eq:induced-decay}
\end{equation}
which decreases as $4|g_{x,\ell}|^2/\gamma_1$ at resonance in the large-$\gamma_1$ limit. These equations fix the coupling convention and motivate the spectral and damping tests below; they are interpretive only and never replace the complete Floquet--Lindblad propagation.

\section{Common product preparation and spectroscopy observables}
\label{sec:observables}

Every principal Gate A v3 calculation begins from
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho(0)&=\left(\ket{\uparrow_z}\bra{\uparrow_z}\right)^{\otimes N}
\otimes\ket{0_d}\bra{0_d},\\[-2pt]
N&=6.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:common-preparation}
\end{equation}
This declaration rules out Floquet-pair, numerical-mode, and topology-dependent preparation selection.

The TLS detector ratio is $r=\omega_d/(\Omega/2)$, where $\Omega=2\pi/T$. The transverse TLS coherence is $c_d(t)=[\expect{X_d(t)}+\ii\expect{Y_d(t)}]/2$. Define the retained-window mean $\overline{c_d}=(t_f-t_{\rm dis})^{-1}\int_{t_{\rm dis}}^{t_f}\dd t\,c_d(t)$. The baseline-subtracted complex subharmonic phasor is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde A_{\rm TLS}(r)&=\frac{2}{t_f-t_{\rm dis}}\int_{t_{\rm dis}}^{t_f}\!\dd t\,
[c_d(t)-\overline{c_d}]e^{\ii\Omega t/2},\\
A_{\rm TLS}(r)&=|\widetilde A_{\rm TLS}(r)|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phasor}
\end{equation}
The raw integrated response is $\Wraw=\int\dd r\,A_{\rm TLS}^2(r)$. The frozen Gate A v3 grid is $r_q=0.880+0.024q$ for $q=0,\ldots,10$. For shape comparisons we use the normalized distance $D(a,b)=\lVert a/\lVert a\rVert_2-b/\lVert b\rVert_2\rVert_2$. For the damping scan, the same baseline-subtracted transform applied to $\expect{Z_0(t)}$ defines the edge phasor $\widetilde A_{\rm edge}$ and $A_{\rm edge}=|\widetilde A_{\rm edge}|$. Its stroboscopic subharmonic component is
\begin{equation}
M_{\pi,{\rm edge}}=\left|\frac{1}{N_{\rm ret}}\sum_{n\in{\rm retained}}(-1)^n\expect{Z_0(nT)}\right|,
\end{equation}
and the reported TLS emission proxy is $P_{\rm em}=\gamma_1\overline{\expect{n_d}}$, with the mean taken over the retained continuous-time samples. A relative phase is obtained from the relevant complex phasors and is reported only where both amplitudes exceed a finite visibility threshold; a phase at vanishing response is not interpreted.

\subsection{Deterministic numerical sensitivity}
\label{sec:window-validation}

The production-drive numerical-sensitivity baseline uses four samples per half step and a $20T$ transient discard. Every damping axis uses the dimensionless relaxation $\gamma_1T$; the completed bare-rate scan is interpreted only at its stated fixed period. \Cref{fig:windows} compares this baseline with two and eight samples per half step and with early $8T$ and late $40T$ discards. The normalized-shape distances for the two sampling variants are $0.00106$ and $0.000311$, with raw-weight ratios $1.00187$ and $0.99911$ relative to the baseline. The early and late window variants give shape distances $0.176876$ and $0.046216$ and raw-weight ratios $1.04122$ and $0.93229$, respectively. The early window exceeds the frozen $D\le0.10$ criterion and is retained as a transient-sensitivity diagnostic; the $40T$ comparison remains below the criterion. These deterministic checks are not statistical repetitions or confidence intervals.

\section{Boundary-selective exact full-model response}
\label{sec:boundary-response}

\Cref{fig:boundary} presents the two primary geometry tests. The held-out same-drive comparison gives $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{OBC}}=2.5261\times10^{-3}$ and $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{PBC}}=2.6486\times10^{-7}$, hence $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{OBC}}/W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{PBC}}=9.54\times10^3$ in its declared direction. No denominator floor or clipping enters these audit ratios; the small denominators are retained finite values. The response is therefore concentrated at the open-chain contacts under one physical preparation and with the full Liouvillian unchanged apart from the contact or boundary condition.

\section{TLS hybridization and dissipative backaction}
\label{sec:hybridization-backaction}

\subsection{Coupling-resolved response doublets}

After the geometry result, interpretive controls, and frozen transfer test are fixed, we use separate scans to ask how the local TLS participates in and loads the response. In the internally resolved interval $g/J=0.06$--$0.12$, the transverse TLS response develops two features around nominal ratio one, as shown in \cref{fig:tls-spectroscopy}. Their full separation grows approximately linearly with the bare contact coupling; a free-intercept fit over the accepted points gives $R^2=0.9982$. At this fixed contact and harmonic, the plotted scale $g_{\rm eff}=g|B_{0\pi}(0,0)|$ is only a constant rescaling of $g$. The scan therefore establishes coupling-dependent splitting in the resolved interval, but does not independently verify the absolute projected matrix element or an all-coupling perturbative law. Unresolved points remain visible and are not silently incorporated into the fit.

\subsection{Loading followed by fast-loss recovery}

The $N=6$ damping scan contains 31 completed rates at fixed $g/J=0.08$, detector ratio one, 80 periods, and a $20T$ discard. With $JT=2.591813939212$, its range is $0.01296\lesssim\gamma_1T\lesssim7.775$. The edge amplitude and stroboscopic edge $\pi$ component are most strongly suppressed near $\gamma_1/J\simeq0.12$ ($\gamma_1T\simeq0.317$), while the TLS emission proxy reaches its broad maximum at weaker damping. At larger damping, the TLS transverse amplitude decreases but the edge response recovers, reaching $A_{\rm edge}=0.641$ and $M_{\pi,\rm edge}=0.541$ at $\gamma_1/J=3.0$.

This attribution is interpretive rather than a quantitative fit, and the crossovers are not phase boundaries.

\section{Reduced multichannel diagnostics and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

A projected edge--TLS or multichannel description can organize sidebands, near-$\pi$ channel structure, and local hybridization intuition, but it is not the primary evidence of this paper. The exact $N=4$ channel/time benchmark provides a controlled bridge between one conjugate channel pair and an independently initialized late-time fit. At $N=6$, the available channel records are edge-seeded, residual-qualified conjugate Ritz pairs near $-1$ with nonzero edge visibility; they are not an exhaustive channel spectrum or a channel/time lifetime-equality test.

The legacy $K$-truncation and $N=6$/$N=8$ comparisons are therefore retained as diagnostics of the projected representation, not promoted to a second positive size-scaling claim. They were not generated under the common-product Gate A contract, and their strong-coupling and branch-selection sensitivity prevents them from validating the full dissipative response by themselves. The sharper failure occurs in the multi-position continuation: matching one edge dressed scale required bare couplings $g=0.08$, $1.60461$, and $27.0097$ at contacts $x=0$, $1$, and $2$, respectively, while the interior spectra did not retain a stable near-resonant two-peak structure across the checked windows. Thus the reduced picture is useful as local mechanism language within a resolved window, but it does not furnish a universal multichannel collapse across contact, coupling, and size. The supporting prepared-pair benchmark, targeted Ritz records, $K/N$ diagnostics, and Gate B2/C1 failure records are retained in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The complete Floquet--Lindblad response supplies a focused finite-system result. Under one physical product preparation, a local dissipative TLS has a transverse response that is much stronger at the open-chain contacts than in the interior and is sharply altered when a held-out comparison changes OBC to PBC. That geometry evidence is the primary claim. The BDI-labelled controls then set its interpretive boundary: they are directional same-period comparisons, not a common-period four-class experiment, and their normalized shapes do not supply a universal $\nu_\pi$ classification. The independently frozen Gate D2 result adds an eight-drive $N=4$-to-$N=6$ raw-weight persistence statement without changing those causal limits. Coupling-dependent splitting and nonmonotonic dissipative backaction provide a separate mechanism layer for how the TLS participates in and loads the finite response. This organization makes the method and the physics serve different roles. The exact full-model response answers the empirical question. Further size scaling, an experimentally calibrated microscopic bath, and a broader common-period control ensemble would be required before elevating the present evidence to a universal spectroscopy rule, a thermodynamic invariant, or a many-body time-crystal phase claim.
