\begin{abstract}
A local dissipative two-level system (TLS) can act both as a spectroscopic probe and as a load on the dynamics it measures. We study this dual role for a finite two-step Floquet Ising chain using exact sparse Floquet--Lindblad propagation, with every principal comparison initialized in the same product state. A same-drive open-versus-periodic-boundary comparison and a six-contact scan show that the frequency-resolved transverse TLS response is strongly concentrated at the open-chain boundary. Same-period BDI-labelled controls further show that the sampled normalized line shapes are not determined by $\nu_\pi$ alone, excluding a universal topological-spectroscopy interpretation of this response. In a separate, prospectively specified eight-drive test, the raw-weight hierarchy observed at $N=4$ is reproduced at $N=6$. Coupling and damping scans reveal resolved hybridization and nonmonotonic backaction: intermediate TLS loss suppresses the boundary response, whereas rapid relaxation restores it. These results establish a drive-dependent, boundary-selective finite-system readout whose probe and backaction roles can be tuned independently of any thermodynamic time-crystal claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Floquet systems; two-level systems; open quantum dynamics; Lindblad dynamics; finite-size response; superconducting circuits

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Local TLS defects are a ubiquitous microscopic source of coherent structure and loss in superconducting quantum devices \citep{martinis2005decoherence,muller2019tls,lisenfeld2019electric,abdurakhimov2022identification}. An engineered lossy two-level ancilla can play the complementary role of a controllable local sensor; related devices use dissipation as a sensing resource \citep{wolski2020dissipation}. In either realization, the same locality that enables readout also allows the TLS to load the dynamics that it measures. Here ``spectroscopic'' refers operationally to the frequency-resolved response of this local two-level degree of freedom; it does not mean that one dissipative response curve directly measures a bulk invariant. This raises a finite open-system question: which TLS response features are genuinely boundary selective, and which are artifacts of initial-state choice, geometry, finite-time analysis, or drive selection?

The two-step Ising drive provides a controlled setting within standard Floquet theory \citep{shirley1965solution,sambe1973steady,bukov2015universal}. Its closed free-fermion representation can carry candidate boundary zero and $\pi$ structures \citep{kitagawa2010topological,jiang2011majorana,thakurathi2013floquet}, including long-lived period-doubled edge response and experimentally accessible Floquet symmetry-protected regimes \citep{yates2021strong,yates2022longlived,zhang2022digital}. Boundary lifetime and bulk absorption have also been studied in undriven chains, and noise can modify the decay of driven edge states \citep{kemp2020boundary,rieder2018localization}. Here we address the local measurement problem with a geometry-controlled calculation in which the probe is initialized physically, propagated with the complete open-system dynamics, and treated as a possible source of backaction. A local observable requires both such a preparation and a nonzero local matrix element; a closed-chain label does not by itself generate a dissipative response. Moreover, in a finite passive Lindblad system, a near-$-1$ channel mode is metastable rather than the persistent spatiotemporal order required of a time-crystalline phase \citep{else2016floquet,keyserlingk2016absolute,macieszczak2016metastability,chen2024spectral}.

The model is intentionally platform independent. It can describe an uncontrolled microscopic TLS defect, but the frequency, contact, and coupling scans are most directly interpreted as an engineered lossy ancilla coupled to a programmable spin chain, including circuit-QED-compatible implementations. Auxiliary loss has also been used to stabilize selected Floquet states in a distinct setting \citep{ritter2025autonomous}. We do not quantize a resonator or derive transmon circuit parameters, so the results should not be read as a device-specific circuit-QED model.

Here we use exact sparse Floquet--Lindblad propagation of an $N=6$ Ising chain coupled to one amplitude-damped TLS. The argument proceeds from phenomenon to interpretation and then mechanism. A movable-contact scan and a held-out OBC/PBC comparison first establish boundary selectivity under one physical preparation. Same-period BDI-labelled controls then delimit what that observation does \emph{not} imply: the sampled normalized line shapes are not fixed by $\nu_\pi$ alone. A prospectively specified eight-drive test asks separately whether a raw-weight hierarchy transfers from $N=4$ to $N=6$. Coupling- and damping-dependent scans finally identify local hybridization and backaction, while channel and multichannel calculations delimit the validity of a reduced description. This ordering keeps the finite response result distinct from any universal topological law.

\section{Floquet Ising chain and dissipative TLS}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Two-step chain and local probe}

The driven chain contains $N$ spin-$1/2$ sites. During one period $T=T_1+T_2$, the two unitary chain steps are
\begin{equation}
H_{ZZ}=-J\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}Z_jZ_{j+1},\qquad
H_X=-h\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}X_j,
\end{equation}
with $T_1=\beta/(2J)$, $T_2=\alpha/(2h)$, and $J=h=1$ in the reported protocol. For OBC, $\mathcal B=\{0,\ldots,N-2\}$; for PBC, the wrap-around bond is included. For PBC the bulk dispersion is
\begin{equation}
\cos(\epsilon_kT)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\cos k\sin\alpha\sin\beta.
\label{eq:bulk-dispersion}
\end{equation}
Thus $k=0$ gives $\cos(\alpha-\beta)$ and $k=\pi$ gives $\cos(\alpha+\beta)$. A zero gap closes on the even branches $\alpha-\beta=2n\pi$ at $k=0$ or $\alpha+\beta=2n\pi$ at $k=\pi$; a $\pi/T$ gap closes on the corresponding odd branches $\alpha-\beta=(2n+1)\pi$ or $\alpha+\beta=(2n+1)\pi$. A TLS of splitting $\omega_d$ is attached at contact site $x$ through transverse exchange. The full step Hamiltonians are
\begin{align}
H_1^{(x)}&=H_{ZZ}-\frac{\omega_d}{2}Z_d+g(\sigma_x^+\sigma_d^-+\sigma_x^-\sigma_d^+),\\
H_2^{(x)}&=H_X-\frac{\omega_d}{2}Z_d+g(\sigma_x^+\sigma_d^-+\sigma_x^-\sigma_d^+).
\label{eq:step-hamiltonians}
\end{align}
The closed-chain BDI pair $(\nu_0,\nu_\pi)$ labels candidate drive controls selected away from the gap-closing lines. It is used to organize the matched controls below, never to construct a dissipative initial state.

\subsection{Amplitude damping and exact one-period channel}

The TLS is coupled to a zero-temperature amplitude-damping bath during both drive steps,
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho=\cL_k^{(x)}\rho=-\ii[H_k^{(x)},\rho]+\gamma_1\cD[\sigma_d^-]\rho,
\qquad
\cD[c]\rho=c\rho c^\dagger-\tfrac12\{c^\dagger c,\rho\}.
\label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
The stroboscopic map is therefore
\begin{equation}
\cE_F^{(x)}=e^{\cL_2^{(x)}T_2}e^{\cL_1^{(x)}T_1}.
\label{eq:floquet-channel}
\end{equation}
All response data in the main text are generated from this complete Floquet--Lindblad model in sparse Liouville space. No reduced edge--TLS Hamiltonian, bare edge wavefunction, or projected coupling is used to create the principal response curves.

The projected edge--TLS equations, sideband convention, and phase convention are given in the Supplementary Material. They are used only to interpret the coupling and damping trends. In particular, no projected matrix element, fitted edge linewidth, or topology-selected state is used to generate the response data reported below.

\section{Common product preparation and spectroscopy observables}
\label{sec:observables}

Every principal full-model comparison begins from
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho(0)&=\left(\ket{\uparrow_z}\bra{\uparrow_z}\right)^{\otimes N}
\otimes\ket{0_d}\bra{0_d},\\[-2pt]
N&=6.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:common-preparation}
\end{equation}
This declaration rules out Floquet-pair, numerical-mode, and topology-dependent preparation selection.

The TLS detector ratio is $r=\omega_d/(\Omega/2)$, where $\Omega=2\pi/T$. The transverse TLS coherence is $c_d(t)=[\expect{X_d(t)}+\ii\expect{Y_d(t)}]/2$. Define the retained-window mean $\overline{c_d}=(t_f-t_{\rm dis})^{-1}\int_{t_{\rm dis}}^{t_f}\dd t\,c_d(t)$. The baseline-subtracted complex subharmonic phasor is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde A_{\rm TLS}(r)&=\frac{2}{t_f-t_{\rm dis}}\int_{t_{\rm dis}}^{t_f}\!\dd t\,
[c_d(t)-\overline{c_d}]e^{\ii\Omega t/2},\\
A_{\rm TLS}(r)&=|\widetilde A_{\rm TLS}(r)|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phasor}
\end{equation}
The raw integrated response is $\Wraw=\int\dd r\,A_{\rm TLS}^2(r)$. The principal detector grid is $r_q=0.880+0.024q$ for $q=0,\ldots,10$. For shape comparisons we use the normalized distance $D(a,b)=\lVert a/\lVert a\rVert_2-b/\lVert b\rVert_2\rVert_2$. For the damping scan, the same baseline-subtracted transform applied to $\expect{Z_0(t)}$ defines the edge phasor $\widetilde A_{\rm edge}$ and $A_{\rm edge}=|\widetilde A_{\rm edge}|$. Its stroboscopic subharmonic component is
\begin{equation}
M_{\pi,{\rm edge}}=\left|\frac{1}{N_{\rm ret}}\sum_{n\in{\rm retained}}(-1)^n\expect{Z_0(nT)}\right|,
\end{equation}
and the reported TLS emission proxy is $P_{\rm em}=\gamma_1\overline{\expect{n_d}}$, with the mean taken over the retained continuous-time samples. A relative phase is obtained from the relevant complex phasors and is reported only where both amplitudes exceed a finite visibility threshold; a phase at vanishing response is not interpreted.

\section{Boundary-selective exact full-model response}
\label{sec:boundary-response}

\Cref{fig:boundary} presents the two primary geometry tests. The held-out same-drive comparison gives $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{OBC}}=2.5261\times10^{-3}$ and $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{PBC}}=2.6486\times10^{-7}$, hence $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{OBC}}/W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{PBC}}=9.54\times10^3$ in its declared direction. Independently, the common-preparation production scan moves the same TLS interaction from contact $x=0$ to $5$. It gives $\Wraw(0)=3.3247\times10^{-3}$, $\Wraw(1)=6.3759\times10^{-4}$, and $\Wraw(3)=3.3875\times10^{-6}$, corresponding to edge-to-neighbor and edge-to-interior ratios of $5.214$ and $9.81\times10^2$. No denominator floor or clipping enters these ratios; the small denominators are retained finite values. The response is therefore concentrated at the open-chain contacts under one physical preparation and with the full Liouvillian unchanged apart from the contact or boundary condition. Numerical-window and sampling-density checks are reported in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Matched controls and limits of the topological interpretation}
\label{sec:bdi-controls}

The control set separates the boundary observation from possible topological interpretations. It contains two strictly same-period comparisons: $(1,1)$ versus $(0,1)$ at $T=2.591813939212$, and $(1,0)$ versus $(0,0)$ at $T=1.295906969606$. Each pair changes $\nu_0$ at fixed $\nu_\pi$; no single common-period line contains all four BDI classes. The full numerical contract, analysis windows, and deterministic sensitivity checks are given in the Supplementary Material.

The response data do not support a universal four-class line-shape classification. \Cref{fig:matched-controls} displays the two same-period control lines, their TLS spectra, directional raw weights, and normalized-shape distances. In the completed data, the within-$\nu_\pi$ distances are $0.6865$ and $0.4661$, while the cross-$\nu_\pi$ distances range from $0.1532$ to $0.7114$. The within- and cross-class distances therefore overlap, so the sampled normalized spectra do not cluster by $\nu_\pi$. The appropriate conclusion is drive dependence, not a universal $\nu_\pi$-determined response line shape or a common-period causal law.

\section{Finite-size transfer of the response-weight hierarchy}
\label{sec:gate-d2}

A prospectively specified finite-size test transfers an eight-drive stratified raw-weight table from $N=4$ to the same tasks at $N=6$. All three predefined conditions are satisfied, and no drive was replaced, added, or retuned after inspection of the $N=6$ response. The internal protocol identifier, task-level values, hashes, and read-only validation record are retained in the Supplementary Material and public repository.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig4_d2_weight_transfer.png}
\caption{Eight predefined drives followed from $N=4$ to $N=6$. Each line joins the same drive ID at two sizes; color denotes its closed-chain $\nu_\pi$ label, and marker shape distinguishes the two drives in each class. Both panels use a common logarithmic raw-weight axis. Within fixed $\nu_0=0$ and $1$, the $N=6$ ratios of the $\nu_\pi=1$ to $\nu_\pi=0$ class medians are $409.54$ and $5633.72$. The minimum $\nu_\pi=1$ weight exceeds the maximum $\nu_\pi=0$ weight by $12.05$. The $\nu_\pi$ strata have different drive periods; lines connect sizes, not matched causal contrasts between labels.}
\label{fig:d2-transfer}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:d2-transfer} displays every transferred point. In this response-blind, cross-period, eight-drive protocol, the raw-weight hierarchy associated with $\nu_\pi$ at $N=4$ is reproduced at $N=6$: the two fixed-$\nu_0$ median ratios are $409.54$ and $5633.72$ (predefined threshold $>10$), and the all-point ordering ratio is $12.05$ (threshold $>1$). This result does not contradict the failed line-shape grouping above. The two analyses concern different observables and different questions: the matched controls compare normalized spectral shapes at fixed period, whereas the transfer test compares raw integrated weights across a stratified set of periods. Persistence of the latter neither makes the former universal nor establishes a common-period causal effect of $\nu_\pi$. It is a finite persistence statement for the sampled hierarchy, not a thermodynamic invariant, a phase boundary, or a universal TLS line-shape experiment.

\section{TLS hybridization and dissipative backaction}
\label{sec:hybridization-backaction}

\subsection{Coupling-resolved response doublets}

After the geometry result, interpretive controls, and finite-size transfer test are fixed, we use separate scans to ask how the local TLS participates in and loads the response. In the internally resolved interval $g/J=0.06$--$0.12$, the transverse TLS response develops two features around nominal ratio one, as shown in \cref{fig:tls-spectroscopy}. For the four accepted points, a free-intercept fit gives $\Delta\omega/J=3.4527(g/J)-0.07333$ with $R^2=0.9982$. Constraining the intercept to zero gives a slope of $2.6852$ and $R^2=0.9459$. The nonzero fitted intercept and the limited resolved range rule out extrapolating either line to a zero-coupling law. At this fixed contact and harmonic, $g_{\rm eff}=g|B_{0\pi}(0,0)|$ is only a constant rescaling of $g$; the scan does not independently verify the absolute projected matrix element. The four residuals and conditional fit errors are reported in the Supplementary Material. Unresolved points remain visible and are not silently incorporated into the fit.

\subsection{Loading followed by fast-loss recovery}

The $N=6$ damping scan contains 31 completed rates at fixed $g/J=0.08$, detector ratio one, 80 periods, and a $20T$ discard. With $JT=2.591813939212$, its range is $0.01296\lesssim\gamma_1T\lesssim7.775$. The edge amplitude and stroboscopic edge $\pi$ component are most strongly suppressed near $\gamma_1/J\simeq0.12$ ($\gamma_1T\simeq0.317$), while the TLS emission proxy reaches its broad maximum at weaker damping. At larger damping, the TLS transverse amplitude decreases but the edge response recovers, reaching $A_{\rm edge}=0.641$ and $M_{\pi,\rm edge}=0.541$ at $\gamma_1/J=3.0$.

For interpretation, eliminating a rapidly relaxing TLS gives the induced edge-decay scale
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm TLS}(\Delta_\ell)=\frac{4|g_{x,\ell}|^2\gamma_1}{\gamma_1^2+4\Delta_\ell^2},
\label{eq:induced-decay}
\end{equation}
which decreases as $4|g_{x,\ell}|^2/\gamma_1$ on resonance at large $\gamma_1$. This scaling motivates, but is not fitted to, the observed recovery.

The nonmonotonic response is consistent with the projected self-energy in \cref{eq:induced-decay}: the TLS loads the finite boundary response at intermediate loss but effectively decouples under rapid relaxation. This attribution is interpretive rather than a quantitative fit, and the crossovers are not phase boundaries.

\section{Reduced multichannel diagnostics and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

A projected edge--TLS or multichannel description can organize sidebands, near-$\pi$ channel structure, and local hybridization intuition, but it is not the primary evidence of this paper. The exact $N=4$ channel/time benchmark provides a controlled bridge between one conjugate channel pair and an independently initialized late-time fit. At $N=6$, the available channel records are edge-seeded, residual-qualified conjugate Ritz pairs near $-1$ with nonzero edge visibility; they are not an exhaustive channel spectrum or a channel/time lifetime-equality test.

The legacy $K$-truncation and $N=6$/$N=8$ comparisons are therefore retained as diagnostics of the projected representation, not promoted to a second positive size-scaling claim. They were generated with a Floquet-pair preparation rather than the common product state, and their strong-coupling and branch-selection sensitivity prevents them from validating the full dissipative response by themselves. The sharper failure occurs in the multi-position continuation: matching one edge dressed scale required bare couplings $g=0.08$, $1.60461$, and $27.0097$ at contacts $x=0$, $1$, and $2$, respectively, while the interior spectra did not retain a stable near-resonant two-peak structure across the checked windows. Thus the reduced picture is useful as local mechanism language within a resolved window, but it does not furnish a universal multichannel collapse across contact, coupling, and size. The supporting prepared-pair benchmark, targeted Ritz records, $K/N$ diagnostics, and internal validation records are retained in the Supplementary Material.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The complete Floquet--Lindblad response supplies a focused finite-system result. Under one physical product preparation, a local dissipative TLS has a transverse response that is much stronger at the open-chain contacts than in the interior and is sharply altered when a held-out comparison changes OBC to PBC. That geometry evidence is the primary claim. The BDI-labelled controls then set its interpretive boundary: they are directional same-period comparisons, not a common-period four-class experiment, and their normalized shapes do not supply a universal $\nu_\pi$ classification. The independent eight-drive finite-size test adds an $N=4$-to-$N=6$ raw-weight persistence statement without changing those causal limits. Coupling-dependent splitting and nonmonotonic dissipative backaction provide a separate mechanism layer for how the TLS participates in and loads the finite response. Finally, the multichannel diagnostics identify a restricted window in which projected language is useful and make its preparation, coupling, contact, and size limitations explicit.

This organization makes the method and the physics serve different roles. The exact full-model response answers the empirical question. Common preparation, predefined controls, and deterministic validation constrain alternative explanations and prevent response-informed selection. Further size scaling, an experimentally calibrated microscopic bath, and a broader common-period control ensemble would be required before elevating the present evidence to a universal spectroscopy rule, a thermodynamic invariant, or a many-body time-crystal phase claim.
