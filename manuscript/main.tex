\begin{abstract}
A local dissipative probe can reveal the response of a driven quantum chain while modifying the dynamics that it measures. We study this interplay in a finite two-step Floquet Ising chain coupled to a lossy two-level system (TLS). Full sparse Floquet--Lindblad propagation starts from the same product state in every principal comparison. At fixed drive, the transverse TLS response is strongly enhanced for open rather than periodic boundaries; moving the contact through the open chain confines the largest response to its edges. Matched same-period controls reveal substantial drive dependence of the normalized TLS spectrum within a fixed closed-chain $\nu_\pi$ class. In a separate, predefined eight-drive test, a cross-period $\nu_\pi$-associated raw-weight hierarchy persists from $N=4$ to $N=6$. Coupling-resolved doublets and a nonmonotonic damping response show that the TLS also hybridizes with and loads the finite boundary response. These results identify a drive-dependent boundary readout with tunable dissipative backaction in a finite Floquet system.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Floquet Ising chain; local quantum probe; open quantum dynamics; boundary response; dissipative backaction

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Local probes turn quantum dynamics into an accessible frequency-resolved signal. Probe-qubit synchronization has been used to infer an environment's spectral density \citep{giorgi2016probing}, while quench spectroscopy has been extended to dissipative lattice models \citep{despres2025quench}. Here we scan the splitting of a local lossy TLS and compare its response at different chain contacts and boundary conditions under one preparation. The coupling that makes this signal visible can also change the system being interrogated. We ask how the TLS spectrum distinguishes the edge of a driven chain from its interior and from a periodic geometry, and how probe loss modifies that distinction. Throughout, ``spectroscopy'' refers to the dependence of finite-time TLS coherence on its splitting.

The two-step Ising drive offers a tractable setting for this question within Floquet theory \citep{shirley1965solution,sambe1973steady,bukov2015universal}. In the closed-chain free-fermion description, zero and $\pi$ boundary structures can be characterized by drive-dependent invariants \citep{kitagawa2010topological,jiang2011majorana,thakurathi2013floquet}. Period-doubled edge response and Floquet symmetry-protected dynamics have been studied in spin chains \citep{yates2021strong,yates2022longlived,zhang2022digital}; Floquet edge states have also been considered as resources for qubit-state transfer \citep{tan2020transfer}. These studies motivate a local readout that resolves the roles of preparation, coupling, probe frequency, and dissipation. Boundary qubits and noisy Floquet edges provide related precedents \citep{kemp2020boundary,rieder2018localization}. We therefore retain the full chain--TLS Lindblad dynamics while controlling preparation and geometry.

Our observables characterize finite-time readout and long-lived modes of a finite open Floquet system, alongside the broader literature on subharmonic order and metastability \citep{else2016floquet,keyserlingk2016absolute,macieszczak2016metastability,chen2024spectral}. The TLS represents a lossy ancilla; defect TLSs in superconducting devices and dissipation-assisted qubit sensing provide possible physical contexts \citep{martinis2005decoherence,muller2019tls,lisenfeld2019electric,abdurakhimov2022identification,wolski2020dissipation}. Dissipative stabilization of Floquet states provides a complementary setting \citep{ritter2025autonomous}.

We address three related questions with exact sparse propagation of the finite Floquet--Lindblad model. First, how does a TLS initialized together with the chain in one product state respond to changes of contact and boundary condition? Second, how do the spectrum and integrated weight vary across closed-chain BDI labels and between two finite sizes under a predefined test? Third, how do coupling and damping reshape the boundary response? The geometry comparison supplies the principal boundary evidence; matched drive controls and the fixed eight-drive $N=4\to6$ test establish the scope of the label-associated trends; coupling, damping, and reduced-channel analyses address the mechanism and its range of validity.

\section{Floquet Ising chain and dissipative TLS}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Two-step chain and local probe}

The driven chain contains $N$ spin-$1/2$ sites. During one period $T=T_1+T_2$, the two unitary chain steps are
\begin{equation}
H_{ZZ}=-J\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}Z_jZ_{j+1},\qquad
H_X=-h\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}X_j,
\end{equation}
with $T_1=\beta/(2J)$, $T_2=\alpha/(2h)$, and $J=h=1$ in the reported protocol. For OBC, $\mathcal B=\{0,\ldots,N-2\}$; for PBC, the wrap-around bond is included. For PBC the bulk dispersion is
\begin{equation}
\cos(\epsilon_kT)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\cos k\sin\alpha\sin\beta.
\label{eq:bulk-dispersion}
\end{equation}
Thus $k=0$ gives $\cos(\alpha-\beta)$ and $k=\pi$ gives $\cos(\alpha+\beta)$. A zero gap closes on the even branches $\alpha-\beta=2n\pi$ at $k=0$ or $\alpha+\beta=2n\pi$ at $k=\pi$; a $\pi/T$ gap closes on the corresponding odd branches $\alpha-\beta=(2n+1)\pi$ or $\alpha+\beta=(2n+1)\pi$. A TLS of splitting $\omega_d$ is attached at contact site $x$ through transverse exchange. The full step Hamiltonians are
\begin{align}
H_1^{(x)}&=H_{ZZ}-\frac{\omega_d}{2}Z_d+g(\sigma_x^+\sigma_d^-+\sigma_x^-\sigma_d^+),\\
H_2^{(x)}&=H_X-\frac{\omega_d}{2}Z_d+g(\sigma_x^+\sigma_d^-+\sigma_x^-\sigma_d^+).
\label{eq:step-hamiltonians}
\end{align}
The closed-chain BDI pair $(\nu_0,\nu_\pi)$ labels candidate drive controls selected away from the gap-closing lines. All dissipative trajectories start from the common physical state specified below.

\subsection{Amplitude damping and exact one-period channel}

The TLS is coupled to a zero-temperature amplitude-damping bath during both drive steps,
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho=\cL_k^{(x)}\rho=-\ii[H_k^{(x)},\rho]+\gamma_1\cD[\sigma_d^-]\rho,
\qquad
\cD[c]\rho=c\rho c^\dagger-\tfrac12\{c^\dagger c,\rho\}.
\label{eq:lindblad}
\end{equation}
The stroboscopic map is therefore
\begin{equation}
\cE_F^{(x)}=e^{\cL_2^{(x)}T_2}e^{\cL_1^{(x)}T_1}.
\label{eq:floquet-channel}
\end{equation}
The principal response curves follow this complete Floquet--Lindblad model in sparse Liouville space. Projected edge--TLS equations, sideband notation, and phase conventions in the Supplemental Material provide an interpretive description of the coupling and damping trends.

\section{Common product preparation and spectroscopy observables}
\label{sec:observables}

Every principal full-model comparison begins from
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho(0)&=\left(\ket{\uparrow_z}\bra{\uparrow_z}\right)^{\otimes N}
\otimes\ket{0_d}\bra{0_d},\\[-2pt]
N&=6.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:common-preparation}
\end{equation}
The chain and TLS start in the same physical state across the principal drive, contact, and boundary comparisons.

The TLS detector ratio is $r=\omega_d/(\Omega/2)$, where $\Omega=2\pi/T$. The transverse TLS coherence is $c_d(t)=[\expect{X_d(t)}+\ii\expect{Y_d(t)}]/2$. Define the retained-window mean $\overline{c_d}=(t_f-t_{\rm dis})^{-1}\int_{t_{\rm dis}}^{t_f}\dd t\,c_d(t)$. The baseline-subtracted complex subharmonic phasor is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde A_{\rm TLS}(r)&=\frac{2}{t_f-t_{\rm dis}}\int_{t_{\rm dis}}^{t_f}\!\dd t\,
[c_d(t)-\overline{c_d}]e^{\ii\Omega t/2},\\
A_{\rm TLS}(r)&=|\widetilde A_{\rm TLS}(r)|.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:phasor}
\end{equation}
The raw integrated response is $\Wraw=\int\dd r\,A_{\rm TLS}^2(r)$. The principal detector grid is $r_q=0.880+0.024q$ for $q=0,\ldots,10$. For shape comparisons we use the normalized distance $D(a,b)=\lVert a/\lVert a\rVert_2-b/\lVert b\rVert_2\rVert_2$. For the damping scan, the same baseline-subtracted transform applied to $\expect{Z_0(t)}$ defines the edge phasor $\widetilde A_{\rm edge}$ and $A_{\rm edge}=|\widetilde A_{\rm edge}|$. Its stroboscopic subharmonic component is
\begin{equation}
M_{\pi,{\rm edge}}=\left|\frac{1}{N_{\rm ret}}\sum_{n\in{\rm retained}}(-1)^n\expect{Z_0(nT)}\right|,
\end{equation}
and the reported TLS emission proxy is $P_{\rm em}=\gamma_1\overline{\expect{n_d}}$, with the mean taken over the retained continuous-time samples. A relative phase is obtained from the relevant complex phasors on the visibility-qualified subset where both amplitudes exceed the stated threshold.

\section{Boundary-selective exact full-model response}
\label{sec:boundary-response}

\Cref{fig:boundary} tests geometry under the fixed product preparation. At the held-out drive, changing only the chain boundary condition reduces the integrated TLS response from $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{OBC}}=2.5261\times10^{-3}$ to $W_{\mathrm r}^{\mathrm{PBC}}=2.6486\times10^{-7}$, a directional OBC/PBC ratio of $9.54\times10^3$. Independently, moving the TLS along the open chain gives $\Wraw(0)=3.3247\times10^{-3}$, $\Wraw(1)=6.3759\times10^{-4}$, and $\Wraw(3)=3.3875\times10^{-6}$: the edge exceeds its neighbor by $5.214$ and this interior contact by $9.81\times10^2$. These ratios use the stored nonzero denominators without a floor. Together, the boundary-condition and position changes establish a strong finite-system boundary contrast in the local readout. The role of the closed-chain BDI labels is tested separately below. Sampling and retained-window checks are detailed in the Supplemental Material.

\section{Matched controls and limits of the topological interpretation}
\label{sec:bdi-controls}

The geometry contrast motivates asking whether a closed-chain BDI label predicts the measured spectrum. We compare two same-period drive pairs: $(1,1)$ versus $(0,1)$ at $T=2.591813939212$, and $(1,0)$ versus $(0,0)$ at $T=1.295906969606$. Each pair changes $\nu_0$ at fixed $\nu_\pi$; the two pairs have different periods. Together they test response differences within the matched lines and empirical grouping across the four sampled spectra. Numerical settings and window checks are in the Supplemental Material.

\Cref{fig:matched-controls} shows that drives sharing $\nu_\pi$ produce distinct normalized responses: their within-label shape distances are $0.6865$ and $0.4661$. Cross-label distances range from $0.1532$ to $0.7114$, overlapping the within-label values. The tested spectra therefore reveal substantial drive dependence within a fixed $\nu_\pi$ class and a weak empirical grouping by that label across this sample.

\section{Finite-size transfer of the response-weight hierarchy}
\label{sec:gate-d2}

The preceding shape test and a size-transfer test address distinct questions. We ask whether the ordering of raw integrated weights observed in an eight-drive $N=4$ table survives when the same drives and detector grids are evaluated at $N=6$. The drives and three pass conditions were specified before inspecting the $N=6$ results; all eight points are retained. The exact tasks, thresholds, and validation record are given in the Supplemental Material.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig4_d2_weight_transfer.png}
\caption{Eight predefined drives followed from $N=4$ to $N=6$. Each line joins the same drive ID at two sizes; color denotes its closed-chain $\nu_\pi$ label, and marker shape distinguishes the two drives in each class. Both panels use a common logarithmic raw-weight axis. Within fixed $\nu_0=0$ and $1$, the $N=6$ ratios of the $\nu_\pi=1$ to $\nu_\pi=0$ class medians are $409.54$ and $5633.72$. The minimum $\nu_\pi=1$ weight exceeds the maximum $\nu_\pi=0$ weight by $12.05$. The label strata span different drive periods; the lines track fixed drive IDs across sizes.}
\label{fig:d2-transfer}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:d2-transfer} shows that the frozen raw-weight ordering passes at $N=6$. At fixed $\nu_0=0$ and $1$, the ratios of the $\nu_\pi=1$ to $\nu_\pi=0$ class medians are $409.54$ and $5633.72$ (threshold $>10$); the minimum $\nu_\pi=1$ weight divided by the maximum $\nu_\pi=0$ weight is $12.05$ (threshold $>1$). The sampled weight hierarchy thus persists across these two finite sizes. Because the label strata differ in period, this test establishes persistence of the predefined ordering rather than a causal effect of $\nu_\pi$ at fixed period. Raw weight and normalized spectral shape capture different aspects of the response, accounting for the different patterns in the two tests.

\section{TLS hybridization and dissipative backaction}
\label{sec:hybridization-backaction}

\subsection{Coupling-resolved response doublets}

The TLS also reshapes the signal: changing its exchange coupling changes the spectrum. In the four internally resolved spectra at $g/J=0.06$--$0.12$, the TLS response develops a coupling-dependent doublet near detector ratio one [\cref{fig:tls-spectroscopy}]. A free-intercept fit over this finite interval gives $\Delta\omega/J=3.4527(g/J)-0.07333$ and $R^2=0.9982$. This fit describes the four resolved points; the nonzero intercept and unresolved weaker-coupling settings delimit its extrapolation. At fixed contact and harmonic, $g_{\rm eff}=g|B_{0\pi}(0,0)|$ is a constant rescaling of $g$. An independent cross-contact test would be needed to evaluate the absolute projected matrix element. The alternative origin-constrained fit, residuals, and window dependence are reported in the Supplemental Material.

\subsection{Loading followed by fast-loss recovery}

Varying only the TLS relaxation rate gives a nonmonotonic boundary response. At $N=6$, $g/J=0.08$, and detector ratio one, the edge amplitude and alternating stroboscopic edge component are most strongly suppressed near $\gamma_1T\simeq0.317$. At larger rates the TLS transverse amplitude decreases while the edge signal recovers, reaching $A_{\rm edge}=0.641$ and $M_{\pi,\rm edge}=0.541$ at $\gamma_1T\simeq7.775$. These values come from 31 completed rates over 80 periods with a $20T$ discard; the emission proxy reaches a broad maximum at weaker damping [\cref{fig:damping-backaction}].

For interpretation, eliminating a rapidly relaxing TLS gives the induced edge-decay scale
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm TLS}(\Delta_\ell)=\frac{4|g_{x,\ell}|^2\gamma_1}{\gamma_1^2+4\Delta_\ell^2},
\label{eq:induced-decay}
\end{equation}
which decreases as $4|g_{x,\ell}|^2/\gamma_1$ on resonance at large $\gamma_1$.

The falloff in \cref{eq:induced-decay} provides a qualitative account of the observed recovery under rapid relaxation. The full-model scan characterizes a finite-rate crossover from loading to recovery. Together with the doublet, it shows that the probe changes the very boundary dynamics whose spectrum it records.

\section{Reduced multichannel diagnostics and limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

A projection onto edge-related Floquet channels offers a physical language for local sidebands and hybridization, provided its range of validity is checked. The exact $N=4$ channel/time benchmark compares one conjugate channel pair with an independently initialized late-time fit. At $N=6$, an edge-seeded Ritz search resolves residual-qualified conjugate pairs near $-1$ with nonzero edge visibility. This targeted diagnostic complements the exact $N=4$ channel/time benchmark.

The $K$-channel and $N=6$/$N=8$ comparisons characterize a separate Floquet-pair-prepared representation. In a spatial stress test, matching one edge dressed scale required bare couplings $g=0.08$, $1.60461$, and $27.0097$ at $x=0$, $1$, and $2$; the interior spectra lost a stable near-resonant doublet across the checked windows. The projected picture captures the resolved edge-contact regime, while the interior continuation identifies its loss of predictive robustness across contacts. Channel benchmarks, truncation audits, and the interior continuation appear in the Supplemental Material.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The central result is a geometry-dependent local observable of a finite driven open system. With the same product preparation, the TLS sees a strong open-boundary response that weakens at interior contacts and in the periodic chain. Geometry establishes boundary sensitivity, while normalized spectral shape retains strong drive dependence within fixed $\nu_\pi$ classes. The frozen eight-drive comparison adds persistence of a cross-period raw-weight hierarchy from $N=4$ to $N=6$. Spectral shape and integrated power are different observables, so these two patterns coexist.

The probe's influence is part of that interpretation. Its coupling produces a resolved doublet over a finite interval, and intermediate loss suppresses the edge response before rapid loss allows recovery. A projected edge--TLS model rationalizes selected edge-contact features and identifies its breakdown in the interior continuation. Together, the geometry, drive, size, and backaction tests establish a finite, drive-dependent boundary readout whose response can be tuned through probe coupling and loss. A larger common-period drive ensemble, controlled size scaling, and a platform-specific bath model offer routes to broader tests.
